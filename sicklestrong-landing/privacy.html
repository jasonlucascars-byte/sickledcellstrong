SickleStrong helps families organize sickle cell care information. This notice explains, in plain language, what we collect, where it is kept, who can see it, and how to remove it. We aim to say only what we can stand behind — where a protection has not yet been independently verified, we say so rather than overstate it.

Three ways your information is handled

It helps to separate three different situations, because they are not the same:

1. Local-only use

If you use SickleStrong on a single device without creating a family account, the care information you enter (such as medications, temperatures, hydration, pain entries, and notes) is stored in that browser on that device. It is not sent to us. Clearing your browser storage removes it.

2. Family account and cloud sync

If you create a family account, the information needed to sign in and to synchronize across devices and approved caregivers is stored in our cloud database provider (Supabase). This lets more than one approved caregiver work from the same family record. Multi-device caregiver sync is being tested during the founding beta; we will tell you clearly which sharing features are active before you rely on them.

3. The public landing page and beta signup

This marketing site collects the limited information described below when you join the founding beta, and — if analytics is enabled — basic, aggregate usage measurement. This is separate from the care data inside the app.

What the beta signup collects

The founding-beta form collects only:

  • your first name;
  • your email address;
  • the care role you select (for example, “one child” or “myself”);
  • an optional “how did you hear about us” note; and
  • your consent to receive beta access and updates, with the time it was given.

If you arrive through a campaign link, we may also record standard campaign tags (UTM source, medium, and campaign) from the web address.

The signup form does not collect health details. It never asks for ages, diagnoses, genotypes, medications, symptoms, or other medical information. We do not sell personal information.

Where information is stored

Beta signups and family-account data are stored with Supabase, our database and authentication provider. Connections between your browser, our functions, and Supabase use HTTPS/TLS encryption in transit. Access to the beta-signup table is restricted at the database level (row-level security), and the key that can read it is held only on the server, never in your browser.

We are still completing our own verification of storage protections — including data at rest, backups, logs, and exports. Until that review is finished and documented, we will not claim “complete encryption,” “absolute security,” “total isolation,” or HIPAA compliance. If you have a specific security question, please ask (see Contact).

Access and sharing

Beta-signup information is used to contact you about beta access and product updates. Within a family account, information is visible to the account owner and to caregivers they approve. We use service providers (such as our hosting, database, and — if enabled — email and analytics providers) to operate the product; they process information on our behalf under their terms. We do not sell personal health information.

Keeping and deleting information

We keep beta-signup information while the beta program is active and while you wish to receive updates. You can ask us to delete your signup or your family-account data at any time by contacting us; we will remove it within a reasonable period, except where we must keep limited records to meet legal obligations. Local-only data is removed by clearing your device's browser storage.

Your choices

  • You can unsubscribe from product updates at any time using the link in our emails or by contacting us.
  • You can request access to, correction of, or deletion of your information.
  • You can choose local-only use and avoid creating a cloud account.

Children's information

SickleStrong is intended for use by parents and caregivers, and by teens or adults managing their own care. When a parent or caregiver creates a child profile, they are responsible for the information they enter about that child. The public beta-signup form is meant for adults and does not request information about children.
